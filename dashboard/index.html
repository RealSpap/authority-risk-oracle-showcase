Every chain tab above reads live, in your browser, straight from getScore(address) on that chain's deployed oracle (selector 0xd47875d0), after enumerating trackedTargets(uint256) (0x9481e5e0) up to trackedTargetsCount() (0x835e2172). Not-applicable sub-scores (e.g. oracleAuthorityScore on a lending vault) read 100 by convention - full marks, not a penalty - rather than being silently omitted. Click a card's Link chip to get a shareable, deep-linkable URL straight to that finding.

The Zcash tab is a genuinely different mechanism, not a smaller version of the EVM one: Zcash has no contract VM, so there's no oracle contract to call. Instead it's a signed, off-chain score attestation with its commitment hash anchored in a real Zcash Testnet OP_RETURN output - this dashboard fetches that transaction, decodes the commitment, and recomputes it from the full attestation live in your browser, rather than trusting either side alone. It now verifies four publications: the ARO2 commitment over the six live targets (the Zenrock zenZEC keyring was retired from the live set on 2026-09-20, see its legacy snapshot), the ARO2 whole-set commitment re-anchored from a rotated key (publisher_key_exposure_2026-09-19.md), the first ARO2 anchor of the same set (testnet_oracle_batch_2026-09-19.md), and the earlier single-score ARO1 proof of concept (testnet_oracle_poc_2026-09-19.md). All four prove integrity and timing of the scores, not that the scores are true, and the last two were signed by a publisher key that was later found in the repository's history.

Cross-exposure is a 5th dimension, separate from the composite: it checks whether the same real signer (a bare EOA, or a Gnosis Safe owner) also controls another tracked target, on the same chain or on another ecosystem, reusing this project's multisig-overlap methodology. 100 = no shared signer with any other group; within one ecosystem it drops by 20 per additional group sharing a root signer, and a root committee identical to a tracked target's committee on another ecosystem is capped at a flat 80. The cap is min(within-ecosystem score, 80), so a value already lowered is never raised back, and a scorer compares the owner set it just read against a dated snapshot of the other chain's committee instead of calling a second RPC (the rule is documented once, in METHODOLOGY.md, under "Convention (decided 2026-09-20)"). As of 2026-09-20 the EVM tabs and Tempo fold the committees they hold a dated snapshot of; a target with no snapshot to compare against (for example Arbitrum's GMX and Camelot, or Plasma's validator set and Yuzu) reports 100, which means no overlap was checked for, not that none exists. These values were pushed on-chain on 2026-09-20 to the Ethereum L1, Arbitrum, Plasma, Monad, Tempo and Robinhood Chain oracles (Base had no score change and was not re-pushed), and each oracle now reads back equal to the scorer output; cards whose numbers changed in that push say "pushed on-chain 2026-09-20". On the Arbitrum and Base tabs this caught a real, live finding: Aave's own emergency-guardian Safe is the identical 9-signer committee on both chains, just redeployed at a different address, so one compromised committee reaches both chains' cancel path.
